This e-portfolio collects the evidence of my work across the twelve units of the Security and Risk Management module: two collaborative discussions, a team risk identification report, an individual quantitative risk assessment, formative activities, feedback, and my professional development plan. It closes with a 1,000-word reflective piece written using Rolfe, Freshwater and Jasper's (2001) *What? So what? Now what?* model.

I work as a Lead Specialist in Cybersecurity, so I came to the module with a practitioner's habits: likelihood-impact matrices, CVSS scores and compliance checklists. The thread running through this portfolio is how the module changed those habits, from treating a risk rating as a fact to treating it as a judgement whose quality depends on the knowledge behind it.

## Learning outcomes
{: #learning-outcomes}

- **LO1.** Identify and analyse critically security risks, threats and vulnerabilities in information systems, accounting for the current threat landscape.
- **LO2.** Gather and synthesise information from multiple sources, including internet security alerts and warning sites, to aid the systematic analysis of risks and security issues, and to manage and audit them.
- **LO3.** Critically determine appropriate methodologies, tools and techniques to mitigate or solve security risks and their business impact.
- **LO4.** Articulate the legal, social, ethical and professional issues faced by information security and risk professionals.

## The module at a glance

The module moved through four stages, and the portfolio follows the same order.

1. **Foundations (Units 1 to 3).** Definitions of risk, the risk management process, qualitative and quantitative assessment, user participation and threat modelling. Collaborative Discussion 1 examined how digitalisation introduces risk, using the maritime human rights case of Hancock *et al.* (2024).
2. **Threats and standards (Units 4 to 6).** Applying threat modelling, selecting security frameworks, GDPR case studies, and the team risk identification report for Pampered Pets.
3. **Quantification and recovery (Units 7 to 10).** Monte Carlo and Bayesian modelling, CVSS and its alternatives in Collaborative Discussion 2, business continuity, and disaster recovery design.
4. **Synthesis and the future (Units 11 and 12).** The individual quantitative risk assessment and disaster recovery recommendation, and the debate on the most influential trend in security and risk management.
