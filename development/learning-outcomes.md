## LO1. Identify and analyse critically security risks, threats and vulnerabilities

**Before the module** I identified risks mainly through technical vulnerabilities and rated them on likelihood-impact matrices or CVSS scores.

**Now** I can identify risks beyond the technical, including social harms, supply chain, capacity and strategic inaction, and analyse them critically, which includes judging how strong the knowledge behind each rating is. I can choose where a method like STRIDE fits and where it does not.

**Evidence:** the four harms in my [Discussion 1 initial post]({{ '/discussions/discussion-1/' | relative_url }}); the current-state assessment I wrote for the [team report]({{ '/projects/team-project/' | relative_url }}), including strategic inaction; the [scoped STRIDE model]({{ '/units/unit-04/' | relative_url }}); fifteen quality and supply risks in the [executive summary]({{ '/projects/individual-project/' | relative_url }}).

## LO2. Gather and synthesise information from multiple sources

**Before the module** I relied on vendor advisories, scanners and standards.

**Now** I combine academic literature, industry surveys, regulatory texts and peer argument, and I state which sources each conclusion rests on. In the executive summary, every probability estimate is labelled as either a survey statistic (Verizon, the UK Cyber Security Breaches Survey, the Business Continuity Institute) or judgement, and I adjusted survey base rates for the firm's size and profile.

**Evidence:** both [summary posts]({{ '/discussions/discussion-2/' | relative_url }}), which integrate peer feedback with the unit readings; the "Basis" column in Tables 1 and 2 of the [executive summary]({{ '/projects/individual-project/' | relative_url }}); EPSS and CISA's SSVC guide in my Discussion 2 summary.

## LO3. Critically determine methodologies, tools and techniques to mitigate risks

**Before the module** I defaulted to the methods I used at work.

**Now** I select methods by the question and the data available, and I can defend the choice against alternatives: Monte Carlo with PERT ranges over qualitative matrices, single-point ALE or FAIR when there is no loss history; SSVC for prioritisation with CVSS as an input; an active-active design for one-minute recovery objectives, delivered through a managed provider because the client could not run it.

**Evidence:** the method section and sensitivity test in the [executive summary]({{ '/projects/individual-project/' | relative_url }}); the [DR design]({{ '/units/unit-10/' | relative_url }}); the [frameworks wiki entry]({{ '/units/unit-05/' | relative_url }}); my [Discussion 2]({{ '/discussions/discussion-2/' | relative_url }}) position on governing SSVC's inputs.

## LO4. Articulate legal, social, ethical and professional issues

**Before the module** my legal knowledge centred on Saudi frameworks such as the NCA controls and the PDPL.

**Now** I can map obligations across UK GDPR, EU GDPR and other jurisdictions, treat legal requirements as go-live gates rather than ranked options, and articulate ethical issues such as consent, surveillance and techno-solutionism. I also recognise the professional duty to correct a client's legal misunderstanding, as with the "new GDPR directive" in the Unit 11 brief.

**Evidence:** [Discussion 1]({{ '/discussions/discussion-1/' | relative_url }}) on surveillance and consent; Recommendation 1 of the [executive summary]({{ '/projects/individual-project/' | relative_url }}); the patient-safety and production-safety cautions in the [frameworks wiki entry]({{ '/units/unit-05/' | relative_url }}).
