## Team contract

Agreed and signed by all four members on 24 August 2026 and sent to the module tutor. It is reproduced here as text. The signed original is not published because it contains signatures.

<div class="artefact" markdown="1">
<p class="artefact-meta">Group 3, Security and Risk Management. Team contract, 24 August 2026</p>

**Goals.** Our goal is to produce a concise, critically evaluated and well-referenced Risk Identification Report that meets the assignment requirements and remains within the 1,000-word limit. We aim to complete the work before the official deadline, apply a consistent risk-assessment methodology and develop our research, critical analysis, communication and teamwork skills.

**Expectations.** Each member is expected to contribute a substantive section, participate in group discussions, respond to messages within 24 hours where possible and complete their work by the agreed internal deadline. Contributions should be academically written, supported by reliable sources, correctly referenced in Harvard style and ready for integration into the final report.

**Policies and procedures.** The team will agree on the methodology, proposed digital transformation and structure before finalising the report. Each member will research and draft their allocated section. Drafts should be shared by 30 August 2026. The complete report will then be reviewed for consistency, repetition, referencing, critical analysis and word count. Important decisions and contributions will be recorded.

**Roles.**

| Member | Role |
|---|---|
| Kaona Hoxha | Project lead; proposed digital transformation risk assessment; identification and evaluation of digital, operational and supply-chain risks; proposed mitigations; compilation, editing, referencing and submission of the final report |
| Noora Aldsani | Summary of recommendations for the business |
| Mohmmed Ahmed Hamza Ali (me) | Current threats, risk evaluation and mitigations |
| Janet Karani | Evaluation of the three percentage claims and final recommendation |

**Consequences.** If a member cannot complete their contribution on time, they should notify the group immediately so that responsibilities can be reorganised. Missed deadlines or insufficient work will first be addressed through direct group discussion. Continued non-participation will be documented, reflected accurately in the individual peer assessment and, where necessary, raised with the module tutor. Disagreements will be resolved through evidence-based discussion and majority agreement.

**Signed:** Kaona Hoxha, Noora Aldsani, Mohmmed Ahmed Hamza Ali and Janet Karani, 24 August 2026.
</div>

**How the contract worked in practice.** The roles and the 30 August draft deadline were kept exactly, and no member needed the non-performance procedure. The clause on agreeing the methodology before finalising was the one that mattered most. The disagreement-resolution clause was used once, when the team settled how firmly to recommend digitalisation. Looking back, the contract recorded *who* would write each section but not a word budget per section, which is why the combined draft ran substantially over the limit. I would add that to the next contract.

## Decisions that mattered

**Framework first.** Agreeing ISO 31000 and a single scale before anyone wrote meant that four separately written sections could be compared and combined. This was the most useful decision the team made.

**How firmly to recommend digitalisation.** This took longer than it should have. Noora argued for a phased, reversible position, Janet challenged treating the 50% uplift as a forecast, and the group settled on a conditional recommendation. I had reservations about the framing but raised them only at review.

**The word limit.** Cutting the combined draft to 1,000 words forced some evidence into shorter statements than the authors wanted. Kaona's integration and editing kept the report coherent.

## My self-evaluation

I rated myself 4 out of 5 on each of the seven self-evaluation criteria (28 out of 35). I attended the online meetings, joining one late because of work commitments, and delivered my section by the internal deadline.

My strengths were bringing practitioner security experience into an academic argument, keeping risk statements evidence-based, scoping the threat modelling honestly, meeting internal deadlines, and commenting on others' work rather than rewriting it.

The skills I committed to improve were writing for a non-specialist reader, raising disagreements early rather than at review, and estimating word budgets per section so less material is cut late. These appear as actions in my [PDP]({{ '/development/skills-pdp/' | relative_url }}).

## My written team evaluation

These are my own answers from the module's peer evaluation form, submitted at the end of Unit 6. The ratings I gave teammates are confidential to the tutor and are not reproduced here.

<div class="artefact" markdown="1">
<p class="artefact-meta">Peer evaluation form, team dynamics section</p>

**How effectively did your team work?**

The team worked effectively overall. Roles were agreed and written into the team contract on 24 August, which meant nobody duplicated work and each section had a named owner. Agreeing the methodology (ISO 31000, with STRIDE limited to the digital threat surface) before drafting began was the single most useful decision, because it gave four independently written sections a shared vocabulary for likelihood and impact. Drafts were shared by the agreed date of 30 August, leaving time for a full consistency, referencing and word-count review before submission. The main friction was the 1,000-word limit: the combined first draft was substantially over length, and cutting it meant some evidence and reasoning had to be compressed into shorter statements than the authors wanted. We also spent longer than necessary agreeing how far the recommendation should commit to digitalisation, which was resolved by evidence-based discussion as set out in the contract.

**Were there any behaviours of your team members which were particularly valuable or detrimental to the team?**

The most valuable behaviour was taking on integration work that was not anybody's individual section. Kaona compiled, edited, checked references and controlled the word count, which is what stopped the report reading as four separate papers. A second valuable behaviour was accepting edits to one's own text without treating them as criticism; this made the review stage quick. Setting an internal deadline ahead of the official one also protected the group, since it left room for review rather than submitting a first draft.

**What did you learn about working in a team that you will carry into your next group experience?**

The clearest lesson is that agreeing the analytical framework before writing matters more than dividing the workload evenly. Once the group fixed ISO 31000 and a single likelihood-impact scale, sections written by different people could be compared and combined; the parts we had not agreed in advance, such as how strongly to recommend digitalisation, were the parts that needed rework. I also learned to build editing time into the plan as a task with an owner rather than assuming it happens after everyone finishes. In my next group project I will push for the framework, the position and the word-count budget per section to be agreed in the first meeting, and I will volunteer earlier for integration work rather than only owning a section.

**My greatest strengths as a team member**

Bringing practical security experience into an academic argument and keeping risk statements evidence-based. I was careful to scope the threat modelling honestly, arguing that STRIDE should be limited to identities, systems and data flows rather than stretched to cover food safety and supplier economics, and to describe ratings as provisional judgements where the case study gave us no incident or margin data. I meet internal deadlines, I give feedback on others' work rather than rewriting it, and I accept edits to my own text.

**The group work skills I plan to improve**

Writing for a non-specialist academic reader. My first draft of the current-state section used practitioner terminology that had to be simplified at review, which used group time. I also want to improve at raising disagreements early: I had reservations about how firmly the recommendation was framed but raised them at the review stage rather than during the planning meeting, when they would have been cheaper to act on. Finally, I want to get better at estimating word budgets per section so that less material has to be cut late.
</div>

## What I will carry into the next team

- Agree the framework, the position and the per-section word budget in the first meeting.
- Treat integration and editing as a task with an owner, not something that happens after everyone finishes.
- Volunteer for integration work, not only for a section.
- Set an internal deadline ahead of the official one, which protected us this time.
