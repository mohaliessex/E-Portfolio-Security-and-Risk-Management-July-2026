The unit applied standards to practical cases, including GDPR case studies and the scope of sector standards such as PCI DSS. The Unit 6 seminar covered GDPR Articles 28, 32, 33 and 34. The team's risk identification report, the module's status document, was submitted at the end of this unit.

### What the unit set out to teach

On completing the unit I should be able to:

- Explain which GDPR regulations are applicable in specific situations.
- Describe which other standards might apply in selected situations.
- Advise on mitigations to help a business meet any of the standards applicable to its specific industry (such as data and privacy (GDPR) or financial (PCI-DSS)).

## Seminar: Security standards

*4 September 2026.* The seminar covered the GDPR principles and the articles most relevant to security and risk work: Article 28 on processors, Article 32 on security of processing, and Articles 33 and 34 on breach notification, illustrated by cases involving missing basic security, legacy systems and absent MFA. It then compared ISO/IEC 27001 and 27701, NIST CSF 2.0, NIST SP 800-53, SOC 2, HIPAA and the CIS Controls, and proposed aligning several frameworks through an inventory, a gap analysis, common controls and continuous evidence (Mazumdar, 2026d).

**How I used it.** I used these articles directly in Recommendation 1 of my executive summary (Articles 28, 32, 33 and 35), and the idea of common controls with continuous evidence shaped my conclusion that ISO/IEC 27001 and Cyber Essentials Plus demonstrate compliance rather than constitute it.

## Learning note

This was the unit where the module stopped being abstract. Regulations I knew mostly through Saudi frameworks at work, such as the NCA's controls and the PDPL, had close equivalents in GDPR, and the case studies showed that the most common failures were basic: missing MFA, unpatched legacy systems and weak processor contracts.
