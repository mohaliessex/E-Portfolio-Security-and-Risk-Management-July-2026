The unit compared qualitative and quantitative assessment, introduced FAIR's decomposition of risk into loss event frequency and loss magnitude (Josey, Hietala and Jones, 2014), and argued that user and stakeholder participation is part of the risk process rather than an add-on (AIRMIC, 2010; Renn, Beier and Schweizer, 2021).

- Discuss the differences between assessment approaches (Qualitative vs. Quantitative).
- Explain the importance of user participation in the risk management process.

## Seminar: The role of AI in risk management

*7 August 2026.* The seminar walked through the five-step risk management process (identify, analyse, evaluate and treat, implement, monitor and review) and compared qualitative and quantitative assessment. Qualitative methods are fast, cheap and usable without data, but prone to bias and vague when several risks share the same rating. Quantitative methods support return-on-investment arguments but need hard data and can create false precision. It closed with the value of user participation in surfacing risks that testing cannot find (Mazumdar, 2026b).

**How I used it.** The warning about false precision is the origin of the team report's decision to call its ratings provisional judgements, and of the intervals and labelled inputs in my executive summary.
