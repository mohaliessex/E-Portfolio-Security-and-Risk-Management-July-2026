The unit distinguished threats from vulnerabilities and surveyed threat modelling methods, including STRIDE, DREAD, attack trees and PASTA, using the Threat Modeling Manifesto (Shostack *et al.*, 2020) and the SEI's summary of methods (Shevchenko *et al.*, 2018).

### What the unit set out to teach

On completing the unit I should be able to:

- Describe a number of threat modelling techniques.
- Advise which technique should be used in specific situations.
- Discuss when techniques should be combined in a hybrid model.

## Artefact: Collaborative Discussion 1 summary post

The summary integrated the peer feedback with the Unit 1 to 3 readings. It argued that an automated system's silence is not evidence of low risk (Aven and Thekdi, 2025), that bias at threat-source identification propagates through every later step (Stoneburner, Goguen and Feringa, 2002), and that structured methods such as STRIDE and attack trees expose spoofing and disclosure harms systematically. It concluded that participation establishes what should be protected and from whom, while structured modelling determines how.

## Learning note

The summary is where the module's readings started to connect for me. I had used STRIDE professionally as a checklist; the readings presented it as one method among several, each suited to different questions. That distinction later shaped my argument in the team project about where STRIDE should and should not be used.
